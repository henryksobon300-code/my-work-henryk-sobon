Why this work matters

From unresolved records to independently checkable results

Some research challenges are not about producing a new formula, but about resolving a specific unknown, detecting a hidden equivalence, or making an existing mathematical fact usable in public tools. My independent contributions have led to concrete changes in externally maintained projects: four duplicate quantum-code entries were identified and removed after outside verification; five code records received verified exact-distance status through an accepted pull request; and two benchmark entries previously marked UNKNOWN were independently confirmed UNSAT and corrected.

A separate SAT benchmark report contains an explicit satisfying assignment for an instance recorded as UNKNOWN, but the proposed upstream dataset correction is still under review. An UNKNOWN label is not, by itself, evidence that every leading solver failed on the instance. The supported claim is narrower: an explicit witness was reported and made available for independent checking.

Additional studies are documented as individual, bounded claims with their own inputs, certificates, source links and outstanding checks. They are not presented as accepted results until that evidence exists.

Experimental research

Verification and reproducibility

Each published record identifies a specific testable claim and makes the available evidence and remaining uncertainties explicit. A submitted result is not the same as an independently accepted result.

Evidence and review standard — attribution, independent verification, reproducibility and public/private evidence boundaries.

How I document results

I aim to state the exact problem and source data, freeze code and inputs, include a replay path and negative controls, and report failed approaches as well as successful ones. Independent confirmation of a concrete result is distinct from evidence that the underlying method is scientifically new.
